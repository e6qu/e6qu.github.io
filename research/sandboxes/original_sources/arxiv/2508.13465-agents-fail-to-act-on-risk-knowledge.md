## Summary
From the ToolEmu group: agents *know* that `sudo rm -rf /*` is dangerous when asked, but fail to recognise the same risk inside a real trajectory and often execute it anyway. The authors call this a knowledge/action gap, show it persists with larger and reasoning models, and mitigate it with an independent risk verifier that abstracts the trajectory before judging it.

## Key points
- Three levels measured: risk knowledge (>98% pass), risk identification in trajectories (drops by >23%), safe execution (<26% pass).
- Gap persists for more capable models and reasoning models such as DeepSeek-R1; scaling does not close it.
- A separate verifier + abstractor cuts risky action execution by 55.3% vs vanilla agents.

## Excerpts
> "while they often answer "Yes" to queries like "Is executing `sudo rm -rf /*' dangerous?", they will likely fail to identify such risks in instantiated trajectories or even directly perform these risky actions when acting as agents." (Abstract)

## Relevance to the talk
A crisp, quotable argument for why "the model will know better" is not a security boundary: the destructive-action risk exists without any attacker, so containment and rollback matter.
