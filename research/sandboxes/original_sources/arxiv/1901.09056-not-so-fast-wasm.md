## Summary
Earlier work had claimed near-native WebAssembly performance on small kernels. Building Browsix-Wasm to run unmodified Unix applications in the browser, the authors run SPEC CPU and find a substantial gap: 45% (Firefox) to 55% (Chrome) slower on average, with peaks of 2.08x and 2.5x. Some causes are fixable code-generation issues; others are inherent to the platform.

## Key points
- Browsix-Wasm: runs unmodified Wasm-compiled Unix applications in the browser.
- SPEC CPU: average slowdown 45% (Firefox) and 55% (Chrome); peaks 2.08x and 2.5x.
- Causes split between missing optimisations and inherent Wasm constraints (e.g. safety checks).

## Excerpts

## Relevance to the talk
Puts a price on language-level sandboxing circa 2019. Helpful when explaining why Wasm wins on startup and density but not necessarily on raw compute.
