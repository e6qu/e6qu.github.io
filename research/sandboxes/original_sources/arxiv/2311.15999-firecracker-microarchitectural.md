## Summary
Firecracker promises container-like speed with VM-like isolation, and AWS includes microarchitectural attacks in its threat model. Testing Spectre and MDS proof-of-concepts, the authors find Firecracker offers little protection on its own, including a Medusa variant that affects Firecracker VMs but not processes outside them, and a Spectre-PHT variant that remains exploitable with recommended countermeasures and SMT disabled.

## Key points
- Reviews Firecracker's isolation model and recommended deployment practices.
- Little protection against Spectre or MDS attacks from the VMM itself.
- Medusa variant threatens Firecracker VMs, not mitigated by AWS-recommended defences.
- Spectre-PHT variant exploitable even with recommended countermeasures and SMT off.
- Conclusion: AWS overstates the inherent security of the VMM; guidance incomplete.

## Excerpts
> "we show that AWS overstates the security inherent to the Firecracker VMM and provides incomplete guidance for properly securing cloud systems that use Firecracker." (Abstract)

## Relevance to the talk
Counterweight to "microVMs solve isolation": hardware sharing remains a channel. Useful when discussing multi-tenant agent sandbox hosting.
