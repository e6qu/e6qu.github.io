## Summary
An early benchmark for indirect prompt injection against tool-using agents. Injected instructions arrive through tool outputs (e.g. a product review, an email) and try to make the agent either harm the user directly or exfiltrate private data. Across 30 agents, injection worked a meaningful fraction of the time, and a simple "hacking prompt" reinforcement roughly doubled success.

## Key points
- 1,054 test cases, 17 user tools, 62 attacker tools.
- Two attack intents: direct harm to users; exfiltration of private data.
- ReAct-prompted GPT-4 was vulnerable 24% of the time; adding a hacking prompt nearly doubled that.

## Excerpts
> "We evaluate 30 different LLM agents and show that agents are vulnerable to IPI attacks, with ReAct-prompted GPT-4 vulnerable to attacks 24% of the time." (Abstract)

## Relevance to the talk
Quantifies the threat in the 2024 generation of agents; motivates containment rather than relying on the model to refuse. Pairs with AgentDojo as the "how bad is it" slide.
