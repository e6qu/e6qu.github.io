## Summary
Extends the CHI 2017 fMRI study ([vance-2017-longitudinal-fmri-habituation.md](vance-2017-longitudinal-fmri-habituation.md)) with a three-week field experiment on Android: users installing apps saw privacy permission warnings. Adherence fell substantially over three weeks with standard warnings; with polymorphic warnings it fell more slowly and stayed high.

## Key points
- Lab: attention to warnings declined over a work week, recovering partly between days.
- Field (real app installs, three weeks): adherence to permission warnings "substantially decreased".
- Polymorphic warnings kept adherence high after three weeks.

## Excerpts
> "habituation— decreased response to repeated stimulation—is a serious threat to the effectiveness of security warnings." (Abstract)

> "Consistent with our fMRI results, users' warning adherence substantially decreased over the three weeks." (Abstract)

> "for users who received polymorphic permission warnings, adherence dropped at a substantially lower rate and remained high after three weeks, compared to users who received standard warnings." (Abstract)

## Relevance to the talk
Field evidence, on permission prompts specifically, that people stop heeding them within weeks. Pairs with Anthropic's 2026 figure that testers caught ~17% of dangerous commands early in a session and ~5% after 50 prompts ([agent-sandboxing/anthropic-2026-08-auto-mode-default.md](../agent-sandboxing/anthropic-2026-08-auto-mode-default.md)).
