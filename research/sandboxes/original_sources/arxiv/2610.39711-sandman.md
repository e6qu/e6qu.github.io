## Summary
A production report from Amazon on running sandboxes for agentic RL at scale on shared Kubernetes. Training jobs request thousands of stateful environments in bursts and leak resources when callers time out. Sandman paces creation, tracks readiness, and keeps cleanup responsibility after clients disappear; a small daemon mounted into unmodified task images keeps files and processes alive between calls.

## Key points
- 3.1 million sandboxes for 122 coding-agent training runs in two weeks; peak concurrency 11,858.
- 10,000-request burst: half usable in 116 s, all in 308 s; 44% faster full delivery than a tuned open-source agent-sandbox (Kubernetes) configuration.
- Median no-op command latency 2.0 ms vs 14.3 ms (agent-sandbox) and 203.5 ms (OpenSandbox).
- Left no sandboxes running at client exit.

## Excerpts
> "Training jobs request thousands of environments in bursts that can overwhelm shared cluster services, while cancelled or timed-out requests can leave resources running." (Abstract)

## Relevance to the talk
Shows the other big consumer of agent sandboxes: RL training, not just interactive coding. Scale numbers (millions of sandboxes) make a good slide on why startup latency, density and cleanup now matter as much as isolation.
