## Summary
Proposes judging an agentic system with another agentic system that reads the whole
trajectory and the workspace, not just the final answer. Introduces DevAI, 55 AI-development
tasks with 365 hierarchical requirements (a DAG) and 125 softer preferences. On three code
agents (MetaGPT, GPT-Pilot, OpenHands) the agent judge agreed with a three-expert human
consensus about 90% of the time, against 60-71% for LLM-as-a-Judge, at about 2.3% of the cost
and time of the human judges.

## Key points
- Judge modules (Section 4.1): graph, locate, read, search, retrieve, ask, memory, planning.
  "ask" "determines whether a given requirement is satisfied". Best combination: graph, locate,
  read, retrieve, ask. Memory was "detrimental, as any errors in previous judgments could lead
  to a chain of errors"; planning was "unstable".
- Agreement (Section 4.2, OpenHands): 92.07% (gray-box) and 90.44% (black-box) vs 70.76% and
  60.38% for LLM-as-a-Judge.
- Cost (Section 4.4): 30.58 USD and 118.43 minutes, "2.29% of the cost and 2.36% of the time
  of Human-as-a-Judge".

## Excerpts
> "we introduce the Agent-as-a-Judge framework, wherein agentic systems are used to evaluate agentic systems." (Abstract)

> "This is an organic extension of the LLM-as-a-Judge framework, incorporating agentic features that enable intermediate feedback for the entire task-solving process." (Abstract)

> "[DevAI] provides richer feedback by uncovering how agentic systems falter during the process instead of just focusing on a single performance metric at the end." (Section 3.1)

> "it provides intermediate feedback that is essential for effective and efficient optimization." (Section 6)

## Relevance to the talk
The judge for the self-improvement loop: per-requirement verdicts say whether a change helped;
reading the trajectory says where the agent faltered. The paper does not itself claim to find
stuck loops or wasted work; MAST (2503.13657) names those failure modes.
