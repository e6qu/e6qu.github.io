## Summary
ToolEmu replaces real tools with an LLM that *pretends* to execute them, so agents can be stress-tested on dangerous scenarios (deleting files, moving money) without building or risking real infrastructure. A second LLM grades the trajectories for risk. It shows that even the safest agents of 2023 took risky actions in roughly a quarter of high-stakes cases.

## Key points
- "Sandbox" here is emulated, not an OS boundary: an LM simulates tool outputs, including adversarial ones.
- Benchmark: 36 high-stakes toolkits, 144 test cases; LM-based safety evaluator quantifies risk.
- Human validation: 68.8% of ToolEmu-identified failures judged to be valid real-world failures.
- Safest agent tested still failed 23.9% of the time.

## Relevance to the talk
Shows the word "sandbox" being stretched in the ML literature: here it means a safe *test* environment, not a containment boundary. Useful contrast slide, and an early quantitative datapoint that agents do destructive things without any attacker present.
