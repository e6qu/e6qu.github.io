## Summary
A HotOS position paper from an OS-research angle: static, hand-written policies and user confirmations (app permissions, ACLs) worked when programs had narrow agency, but generalist agents act in too many contexts. Whether "delete email" is safe depends on the task and content. Conseca proposes generating just-in-time, context-specific, human-verifiable security policies per task.

## Key points
- Safety of an action depends on context (goal, content, account type), not just the action.
- Static policies and confirmation prompts "while imperfect, have sufficed" for limited-agency software, not for generalist agents.
- Conseca: just-in-time, contextual, human-verifiable policies.

## Excerpts
> "Judging an action's safety requires knowledge of the context in which the action takes place." (Abstract)

> "manually crafted policies and user confirmation (e.g., smartphone app permissions or network access control lists), while imperfect, have sufficed to restrict harmful actions." (Abstract)

## Relevance to the talk
Frames the central open problem: who writes the sandbox policy for a general-purpose agent? Links mobile permission history to agent permission design.
